1. Executive Summary
GigSync is a web-based career assistance platform that helps job seekers turn their existing experience into a structured resume, discover jobs that fit their profile, understand why they do or do not match a role, and identify practical learning resources to close skill gaps. The product combines document parsing, editable resume generation (including LaTeX/PDF export), explainable job matching, and personalized course recommendations.
The platform is intended to support career decisions—not make hiring decisions or guarantee employment. AI-generated content must be grounded in information provided by the user or clearly marked as a suggestion. The system must never invent degrees, work experience, skills, certifications, metrics, or achievements.
2. Problem Statement
Students and early-career applicants often struggle to prepare professional resumes, find suitable openings across multiple sources, and understand the gap between their current skills and a job description. Job requirements are written inconsistently, and applicants may not know which missing skills are important or how to learn them efficiently. GigSync brings these tasks into one guided workflow.
3. Product Vision and Goals
3.1 Vision
Help users make informed career moves through accessible resume tools, transparent job matching, and actionable learning plans.
3.2 Goals
Convert uploaded resumes or user-written career details into an editable, structured profile.
Generate a clean resume from text or profile data, with LaTeX source and PDF export where supported.
Recommend jobs using evidence from the user's profile and the job description.
Explain matched skills, missing requirements, preferred skills, and possible eligibility constraints.
Recommend relevant courses or learning resources and organize them into a practical roadmap.
Keep users in control of their personal data and all final resume content.
3.3 Non-goals for the initial MVP
Automatically applying to jobs or sending messages to employers.
Guaranteeing interviews, job offers, or job-match accuracy.
Providing paid course hosting or building a full learning-management system.
Scraping websites in ways that violate their terms or access restrictions.
Making automated hiring, rejection, or candidate-ranking decisions for employers.
Supporting every resume format, language, country, and job portal in the first release.

5. Core User Journey
User creates an account or enters the product in a supported guest mode.
User uploads a PDF/DOCX resume or enters details manually.
GigSync extracts candidate information and displays a review screen so the user can correct parsing errors.
User edits the profile and chooses a resume template; the system produces a preview and exportable output.
User searches or browses available jobs and receives a relevance score with reasons.
User opens a job to see matched requirements, missing skills, preferred skills, and any stated hard constraints.
GigSync recommends courses/resources and a staged learning roadmap for trainable gaps.
User saves jobs, learning resources, and resume versions for later review.
6. Functional Requirements
6.1 Account and Profile
FR-01: Users can register, sign in, sign out, and reset credentials using the chosen authentication provider.
FR-02: Users can create and edit contact details, education, experience, projects, skills, certifications, links, and career preferences.
FR-03: The profile must distinguish user-confirmed facts from AI-suggested wording.
FR-04: Users can view, edit, export, and request deletion of their stored profile data.
6.2 AI Resume Builder
FR-05: Accept supported PDF and DOCX resume uploads, plus manual text/profile entry.
FR-06: Extract structured fields such as education, experience, skills, projects, certifications, and links; show confidence or review flags for uncertain fields.
FR-07: Provide a review-and-correct step before extracted information is used to generate a resume.
FR-08: Generate professional resume content from confirmed user data, with selectable templates and editable sections.
FR-09: Support text-to-LaTeX generation and provide the LaTeX source when compilation is successful.
FR-10: Export the resume to PDF; DOCX export may be included if feasible.
FR-11: Allow users to create a job-specific resume version without changing the master profile.
FR-12: Never fabricate qualifications, employment dates, employers, certifications, achievements, or numeric impact.
FR-13: Warn users when a generated statement adds unsupported detail or when important information is missing.
6.3 Job Search and Matching
FR-14: Display job listings from an authorized API/feed, a permitted public dataset, or clearly labelled curated/demo data.
FR-15: Support filters such as title/keyword, location, remote/hybrid/on-site, experience level, and source when those fields are available.
FR-16: Compare the user's confirmed profile with job description requirements.
FR-17: Show a match score with a short explanation; do not present the score as a probability of being hired.
FR-18: Break match evidence into matched skills, missing essential skills, missing preferred skills, relevant experience, and unknown information.
FR-19: Identify hard constraints stated by the listing (for example, mandatory licence or work authorization) separately from learnable skills.
FR-20: Show source, original posting date if available, and an external application link; label stale or unverified listings.
FR-21: Let users save/bookmark jobs and mark them as interested, applied, or not relevant.
6.4 Skill-Gap Analysis and Learning Recommendations
FR-22: Convert job descriptions into a structured list of skills and requirements, preserving essential/preferred distinctions where possible.
FR-23: Compare extracted requirements against the user's confirmed skills and evidence in projects/experience.
FR-24: Explain each identified gap and why it matters for the selected role.
FR-25: Recommend relevant courses, tutorials, documentation, or projects from a curated or authorized source.
FR-26: For each resource, show title, provider, topic/skills covered, level, estimated duration when available, cost/free status when verified, and link.
FR-27: Generate a practical learning roadmap ordered by importance and prerequisites; let users adjust priorities.
FR-28: Clearly distinguish verified resource metadata from AI-generated summaries or estimates.
FR-29: Allow users to mark a resource or skill as planned, in progress, or completed.
6.5 Dashboard and Saved Items
FR-30: Provide a dashboard with the current profile completion state, saved jobs, resume versions, and learning progress.
FR-31: Users can revisit previous resume versions and saved job analyses.
FR-32: Provide clear empty states when no jobs or courses are available rather than displaying fabricated data.
7. AI Behaviour and Matching Logic
7.1 Resume processing
Use document extraction for text retrieval, then an LLM or structured parser to map content into a defined schema. Validate output against the schema and show uncertain fields for user review. AI may improve clarity and grammar but must not add unsupported facts.
7.2 Job match score
The MVP may use a transparent weighted score based on skill overlap, experience relevance, education/certification requirements, and role/title similarity. Weights should be configurable and tested against a small, manually reviewed evaluation set. A semantic model may improve matching, but the interface must explain the principal evidence behind each result. Unknown fields must be treated as unknown—not automatically as a mismatch.

7.3 Recommendation principles
Recommend resources tied to specific missing skills, not generic course lists.
Prioritize essential skills and prerequisite topics before optional enhancements.
Prefer trustworthy, accessible resources and verify links/metadata where possible.
Do not claim that completing a course guarantees qualification or employment.
Let the user decide whether a gap is relevant to their career goal.

11. Non-Functional Requirements
NFR-01 Usability: Core tasks should be understandable to a student with basic computer skills; use plain-language explanations and visible progress states.
NFR-02 Performance: For typical supported resumes, parsing and preview generation should provide clear progress and aim to complete within a reasonable interactive wait; benchmark before setting a firm SLA.
NFR-03 Reliability: Handle malformed, scanned, encrypted, or unsupported documents with actionable errors rather than crashing.
NFR-04 Security: Use HTTPS in deployment, secure authentication, server-side authorization, input validation, secrets management, and safe file handling.
NFR-05 Privacy: Obtain consent before processing uploaded resumes with external AI providers; explain storage and deletion; do not expose one user's data to another.
NFR-06 Accessibility: Use readable contrast, keyboard-accessible controls, labels, and responsive layouts.
NFR-07 Explainability: Every match result should provide evidence and limitations, not only a score.
NFR-08 Maintainability: Keep parsing, matching, resource catalogues, and UI logic modular and testable.
NFR-09 Data quality: Show source and freshness where available; avoid presenting demo data as live listings.
NFR-10 Responsible AI: Minimize sensitive data, avoid inferring protected traits, and provide user correction and override controls.
12. Security, Privacy, and Responsible AI
Treat resumes as personal data. Explain what is extracted, where it is processed, and how long it is retained.
Do not use resume data to infer sensitive personal attributes or to make high-impact decisions on behalf of employers.
Use external AI services only after reviewing their data-handling terms and configuring appropriate privacy settings.
Validate uploaded file type and size; scan or safely process files; never execute uploaded content.
Escape user-provided text in HTML and carefully validate content passed to LaTeX to prevent injection or compilation abuse.
Keep API keys and secrets out of source control; enforce access checks on every user-owned resource.
Use only job listings and course information obtained through permitted sources, and honour source terms and attribution requirements.
Label generated summaries, estimated durations, and uncertain extractions; allow users to correct errors.

14. Acceptance Criteria
A user can upload a supported PDF/DOCX or enter profile details manually and review extracted information before use.
The resume editor allows users to correct content, preview the selected template, and export a PDF; LaTeX source is available when generated successfully.
The system does not add unconfirmed education, employment, skills, certifications, or achievement metrics to the resume.
A job result displays its source and application URL, and the interface clearly distinguishes live/verified listings from demo or stale records.
A match result includes matched skills and missing requirements; essential and preferred gaps are not presented as equivalent.
Course recommendations map to at least one identified gap and include a usable link or are clearly labelled as unavailable.
Users can save a job and revisit the associated analysis.
A user cannot access another user's profile, resume, saved jobs, or learning progress.
Malformed or unsupported uploads produce a clear error and do not crash the application.
Empty results are handled honestly; no fake jobs, course records, match results, or success messages are displayed.

18. Testing Strategy
Unit tests: file parsing, profile validation, requirement extraction, score calculation, access-control helpers.
Integration tests: upload-to-profile, profile-to-resume export, job-to-match analysis, gap-to-course recommendation.
Security tests: cross-user access, malformed files, unsafe LaTeX characters, file size/type validation, secrets exposure.
AI evaluation: manually reviewed resume fields, job requirements, match explanations, and recommendation relevance.
Usability tests: ask representative students to complete resume creation and job-gap tasks; record confusion and completion time.
Regression tests: maintain a small set of sample resumes and job descriptions to detect quality changes.
19. Assumptions and Open Decisions
Initial audience is students, fresh graduates, and early-career applicants; geography and supported languages are to be finalized.
The first release is a responsive web application.
The team must choose one backend framework (Django REST Framework or FastAPI) before implementation.
The team must confirm which job source/API is permitted and available; do not promise live comprehensive coverage until validated.
The team must decide whether authentication is required for the academic demo or whether a limited guest flow is useful.
The team must select an AI provider/model after considering cost, latency, structured-output support, and privacy terms.
Course recommendations initially use a curated or authorized catalogue rather than an unverified open-ended scrape.
The exact match-score weights and evaluation dataset must be documented as implementation decisions, not treated as universal truths.
20. Definition of Done
All P0 requirements are implemented or explicitly documented as deferred with mentor approval.
Core workflows are tested from end to end.
The app handles parsing failures and unavailable external sources gracefully.
The project report describes architecture, algorithms, data sources, testing, actual results, and limitations accurately.
The demo contains no misleading dummy records presented as real data.
Privacy, consent, attribution, and responsible AI limitations are documented.
